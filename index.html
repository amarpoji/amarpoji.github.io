/ 02 LangGraph Multi-Agent Pipeline

Master Advisor

This is a personal project I built to automate my search for a master’s program and, more importantly, a university that can fully sponsor my studies. The story actually starts with my mom. She kept asking me, “So, have you found a university yet?” She wanted me to continue my studies and told me that my father was willing to pay for it since I don’t have a stable income yet. But I didn’t really want to depend on my family for this. I told her I would try to find a fully funded opportunity instead.

That’s when I thought, why not automate the whole search? I built an AI-powered research system that searches for master’s programs, universities, and funding opportunities based on my criteria. While working on it, I discovered LangGraph, which introduced me to a different way of building AI agents. Instead of the agent simply stopping after one search, LangGraph lets the system loop back and forth between agents, creating loops where the system can go back, refine its search, and improve its results. So what started as my mom repeatedly asking me to find a university turned into a pretty interesting AI project.

Hadith Research Agent

As a student of Islamic knowledge, I know how hard it is to find authentic hadith on a topic — the collections span thousands of books. Relying on LLMs alone is risky: a single hallucinated word in the matn can distort the narration. This agent searches trusted sources (sunnah.com, shamela.ws) and provides every source link for verification, cutting research time while keeping reliability.

03 / The Story

From Madinah to machine learning.

My journey has taken a few turns I definitely didn’t see coming. Back in high school, I was firmly in the science stream, studying Additional Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology. I was pretty sure I would end up becoming a doctor or an engineer. At the time, my future seemed nicely planned out. Then, after SPM, my mother offered me the opportunity to perform Umrah. I ended up going with my eldest brother. I went to Madinah expecting a spiritual journey. I didn’t expect it to completely change the direction of my life. While I was there, I met someone named Ahmad at a small grocery store near the Prophet’s Mosque. There was just one tiny problem. I didn’t speak Arabic, and he didn’t speak Malay. 😄 Somehow, we still managed to become friends. We kept in touch through WhatsApp, with Google Translate doing most of the heavy lifting in our conversations. That simple friendship sparked something in me. I became curious about Arabic, started learning it seriously, and before I knew it, the dream of becoming a doctor or engineer had quietly been replaced by a new interest in language and its sciences.

A few years later, I earned a Diploma in Arabic Language and Literature from UniSZA, followed by a Bachelor’s degree in Theology from the Islamic University of Madinah. But apparently, my journey wasn’t done changing direction. My old love for mathematics and problem-solving eventually came back, and this time I discovered data science. It felt like finding a missing piece of the puzzle, something that connected my analytical background with my growing interest in technology. Since then, I’ve been diving into machine learning, deep learning, and AI, experimenting with different ideas and building projects along the way. Looking back, it’s funny how one trip to Madinah, one random encounter at a grocery store, and a lot of Google Translate somehow led me from dreaming about medicine to building AI systems.
